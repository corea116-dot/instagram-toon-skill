# Dialogue Style

- Write natural contemporary Korean suitable for a mobile screen.
- Use short spoken clauses and concrete everyday vocabulary.
- Keep the explicit object and action when shortening would make the meaning ambiguous. Prefer `ISA 계좌를 개설하기 전에` over `열기 전` in explanatory finance copy.
- Do not use generic numbered-checklist phrases unless the nearby text names the actual checks and tells the reader what to do.
- Keep at most two bubbles per panel; prefer one when the beat is visually clear.
- Let the picture carry action, place, and emotion. Do not restate visible information.
- Make the setup precise, the escalation brisk, and the final line the shortest decisive line.
- Use punctuation sparingly. Avoid forced line breaks; the composer wraps text deterministically.
- Preserve intentional fragments, repetitions, pauses, laughter, and character-specific register; they are not automatically errors.
- Preserve allowed roughness: concise, blunt, or mildly sarcastic non-profane Korean may remain natural to the character. Do not flatten it into formal Korean.
- Reject profanity, disguised or initial-only profanity, slurs, sexualized insults, dehumanizing labels, identity/appearance/protected-trait attacks, and humiliating nicknames in both dialogue and captions.
- Treat a naturalness review as a wording-only gate. Do not change facts, comic timing, speaker, bubble count, or bubble geometry.
- Avoid hashtags, emoji, decorative symbols, unexplained slang, mock dialect, and humiliating nicknames.
- Match character-specific voice rules in `character-bible.json`.
- Store displayed dialogue only in `script.json`. Never place Korean or any other lettering in an image-generation prompt.
